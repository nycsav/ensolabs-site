## Rule 3 — Pace, so the account survives
LinkedIn restricts accounts that burst connection requests, and Sav's profile is both the business's front door and his job-search channel. **Cap: 5 requests per day, weekdays only, sent by hand.** 38 leads ÷ 5/day ≈ 8 working days of sending across 4 calendar weeks. Never batch-send. Never use an automation tool against LinkedIn.

## Rule 4 — The note formula
Every note is under 300 characters and does three things in order:

1. **Name their dated trigger** — proves this isn't spray.
2. **One VERIFIED proof point** — see the approved list below. Never name the confidential client.
3. **A peer ask, not a pitch** — "worth comparing notes?" outperforms any offer at the connection-request stage.

Never mention pricing, engagement length, or the 12-Week Pilot-to-Production in a connection request. That comes only after they accept.

## Rule 5 — After they accept
Do **not** pitch on acceptance. Wait 48 hours, then send one message that references something specific and asks a real question. The offer only appears if they ask what Enso does. Log every acceptance in Notion.

## Rule 6 — Tracking: the funnel already exists, nothing has ever entered it
The Notion `Status` field is already built with the right stages:

`New → Enriched → Outreach Sent → Connected → Nurture`

**Every one of the 39 leads is stuck at "Enriched."** The pipeline was designed correctly and then never used. This is not a tooling gap — it is an unworked queue.

Enforce from here:
- On send → move to **Outreach Sent** (same minute, or the record lies).
- On accept → move to **Connected**, and the 48-hour follow-up clock starts.
- 14 days in `Outreach Sent` with no acceptance → **Nurture**.
- Anything in `Connected` with no follow-up after 3 days escalates to the top of the next report.

## What I can automate, and what I won't
**Automated end to end:** discovery, verification, scoring, account clustering, send-order sequencing, note drafting, status tracking, daily "send these 5 today" queue, follow-up timing, and stale-lead escalation.

**Not automated — deliberately:** the send itself. Two reasons, and the second is the one that matters. Bulk-automated connection requests are the fastest way to get a LinkedIn account restricted, and this account is both Enso's front door and Sav's job-search channel — that is a bad trade for saving five minutes a day. Second, a connection request from Sav should come from Sav; the accept rate on these notes depends on them reading as a person, not a sequence.

The daily cost of the manual step is roughly **four minutes**: open the queue, paste five notes, click send.
